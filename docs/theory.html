Read the argument in order

The core theory is an auditable sequence—not a magic self-upgrade.

The guiding question is whether a mathematical discovery system can improve its own methods while preserving verifier-backed meaning, paying every required cost, and transporting its evidence to a successor.

Interpretation rule: A manuscript-level theorem, verified finite model, checker-accepted certificate and deployed improvement are four different evidentiary claims. This library keeps them distinct.

W2 · Future-sufficient revision quotients

Partition states by their contract-relevant labels, then repeatedly split a block if states have different charged successor-block signatures. The resulting strong stable partition preserves the explicitly modeled forcing value, not arbitrary properties of a real machine.

W3 · The price of deciding which revision to make

When the true state is hidden, a policy must act on permitted observations rather than on omniscient knowledge. Finite belief-game translation makes the forcing value explicit. Better observations can help, but the receipt's own cost can erase the benefit.

Read the W3 manuscript

Successor, fixed point, and economics

Revisions should be charged for construction, checking, migration and future workload—not just an isolated benchmark. The evidence that justifies a revision must survive handoff. A mathematically stationary rule or low-cost basin does not, by itself, imply a deployed system has self-improved.
